# What good IDEMS activity write-ups look like (David W, Chiara, Elena, Esmee)

Read-only study. Nothing in Drive was modified. Method, counts and caveats are at the end.

## Headline

- Gabe's rule (small >= 3 sentences, large >= 3 paragraphs) is roughly right as a **floor**. It is too low as a **target**.
- Across the 152 substantive entries by the four authors: **median 6 sentences, 141 words, 2 paragraphs.** 139 of 152 (91%) have 3 or more sentences. Only 67 of 152 (44%) have 3 or more paragraphs.
- Flagship entries (the ParentText / ParentApp / Global Parenting Initiative family, n=30): **median 11 sentences, 245 words, 3 paragraphs.**
- Suggested numbers:
  - **Small initiative: 4 to 6 sentences (about 80 to 140 words), one paragraph.** Never fewer than 3.
  - **Flagship: 9 to 15 sentences, 3 or more paragraphs (about 250 to 400 words).**
  - The unit that matters is the sentence count. Paragraph count is unreliable, because bullet lines inflate it.

## 1. Per-author numbers

"Activity-level" is an entry nested inside another entry (a country deployment, a sub-product, a single event). The reports do not label this split, so it is my heuristic. The line between the two is blurry: Elena's "ParentText" sub-entries sit on the activity side.

Distribution over all 152: sentences p10=3, p25=4, median 6, p75=10, p90=13. Words p10=60, p25=84, median 141, p75=251, p90=312.

How I attributed each entry:

- The 2023 to 2025 Activities docs say so in their instructions: "Capitalise name of the person who wrote the entry". An entry counts as theirs when CHIARA, ELENA, ESMEE or DAVID W is capitalised on the names line under the heading.
- The 2022 to 2024 docs also use the tag `<David W bullets - Elena text>`. I credited **Elena as writer**, and David W as bullet-supplier. These count under both names in the table, so the "all four" row is a union and does not double count.
- 2021 draft: names are not capitalised, only listed ("Esmee (Parent App), Chiara (Parent text)"). I credited only 2 entries (Chiara's) and treated the rest as unattributed.
- The published CIC and Annual Reports (2018 to 2025) carry **no per-entry authorship**. The `(DS + DW)` file suffix on the 2022 Annual Report covers only the directors' narrative. All entry-level data therefore comes from the "IDEMS Activities 20xx" working Google Docs, which feed those reports.

Unsure:

- **David W:** his own capitalised entries are mostly empty (11 of 16 in 2025) or are bullets that Elena then wrote up. His prose is not a usable sample on its own. The best "David W" content is really Elena's text.
- **Elena rewrite of 2022:** many 2022 and 2023 entries were originally drafted by Stephen (a non-listed author). Elena's names appear because she reviewed and rewrote them in Nov 2024. Where "Elena reviewed" appears without capitals or "Elena text", I did not credit her.
- **Entries led by someone else:** e.g. the 2024 PICSA entries are headed "JAMES, Roger <Elena text - ready to review>". I credited Elena, but James drafted the underlying material.
- **Ambiguous names:** "DAVID" alone is ambiguous between David S and David W. I did not count it.
- **Chiara and Esmee** have small samples (Esmee only 4 entries). Their medians are indicative only.

## 2. Small versus flagship

Small initiatives, 3 to 5 sentences, one paragraph, 50 to 110 words. They state what it is, what was done in the year, and what is next:

- Elena's 2022 "Staffing database": 93 words, 4 sentences.
- Esmee's 2022 "IDEMS website": 89 words, 4 sentences.
- Elena's 2024 "Rohingya Text - Malaysia": 91 words, 4 sentences.
- Elena's 2023 "Social Enterprise UK": 3 sentences. This is the floor. It lists events attended and nothing else.

Mid entries are 6 to 9 sentences and 140 to 220 words, in 1 to 3 paragraphs.

Flagship entries are 10 to 20 sentences and 250 to 450 words, in 3 to 6 paragraphs:

- Chiara's 2025 ParentText entries (n=8 country deployments): 194 to 395 words, 9 to 18 sentences, 3 to 5 paragraphs each.
- Elena's 2024 "Global Parenting Initiative": 440 words, 19 sentences, 11 lines.
- The largest, Elena's 2023 "Global Parenting Initiative" (653 words) and Chiara's 2022 "ParentText/RapidPro pipelines" (795 words), are bulleted and over-long. Do not treat them as the target.

## 3. What the best entries contain that thin ones omit

I compared entries with 8 or more sentences against those with fewer than 4. The figures are the share of entries containing each feature (a keyword check, so approximate).

| Feature | 8+ sentences (n=60) | 4 to 7 (n=59) | under 4 (n=33) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers (counts, dates, amounts) | 93% | 83% | 48% |
| Named place or country | 73% | 42% | 21% |
| Named partner or organisation | 78% | 51% | 33% |
| Month-level dates (e.g. "19 May", "1 August") | 55% | 49% | 15% |
| Challenges or learnings | 53% | 34% | 9% |
| Forward-looking close ("next year we will...") | 22% | 12% | 12% |
| Funder or contract language | 43% | 25% | 27% |
| Links | 2% | 0% | 0% |

I dropped a "scale" metric because the regex was faulty.

In prose, the good entries contain:

- **Why it exists and who it serves.** Examples: "huge demand for parenting resources...", "to enable new-joiners to get up to speed", "audio-only chatbot for Rohingya parents... living as refugees".
- **Where.** A country, institution or site (Wolseley, Kuala Lumpur, six named stations in Mozambique).
- **What IDEMS specifically did, as opposed to what the project did.** Examples: "IDEMS developed an initial five-day prototype", "IDEMS established its own RapidPro hosting".
- **What changed or what the result was**, with numbers: 150 participants recruited; about 25,000 join attempts in mid-June forcing a suspension; R50 incentive; USD 0.86 per user; 80 families.
- **Partners.** UNICEF, PLH, KEMAS, WFP, INAM, Oxford. Funder is often in the heading ("PLH - PPFA Funding", "UNICEF Funding") or in a "Funded by:" line.
- **Status and dates.** "Launched 19 May", "released on 1 August", "closed 31 December 2024", "launch rescheduled for January 2026".
- **A candid problem and how it was handled.** Examples: Sri Lanka "did not result in a deployed chatbot"; the WashText survey error.
- **A closing sentence** on outlook or status.

The thin ones typically give only a heading, or a sentence like "attended events including...". They omit the why, the result, numbers and any problem. Links are rare in entries by these four authors (about 1%).

The older published-report format (2018 to 2021) adds a date line ("September 2019 – present") and a "Funded by: University of Oxford" line above the narrative. That is a ready-made structure for the Collaboratorium fields.

## Writing characteristics, tone and level of detail

- **Voice:** institutional first-person plural ("we", "IDEMS") in past tense for what happened, present tense for ongoing status. It is plain and factual, with almost no marketing adjectives.
- **Shape of an entry (Chiara's 2025 pattern):**
  - Sentence 1 summarises the year's focus.
  - Then a chronological walk-through (early year, mid-year, later).
  - Then outcomes, then status or what is next.
  - Often split into 3 paragraphs: planning, build, deploy/result.
- **Shape of a small entry (Elena, Esmee):** purpose, then what was done, then a candid assessment or next step. Esmee's website entry admits "the website has not been kept as up-to-date as we would like" and commits to a fix.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Names, numbers, dates and places stand where an adjective would be (e.g. "five lessons over 10 days", "R50"). Acronyms are expanded on first use (PICSA, UoR (University of Reading), INAM).
- **Honest:** setbacks and failures are stated plainly and dated, and the response is described. Examples: the 25,000-user surge forced suspension and revised safeguarding; the budget constraint meant not all courses were translated.
- **Level of detail:** enough that someone outside the project can answer who, where, what happened, how big, and what state it is in. They do not go into implementation minutiae, and the best entries avoid bullet lists. Entries over about 450 words tend to turn into logs.
- **Weaknesses to avoid:**
  - Elena's rewrites of David W's bullets sometimes read as generic ("comprehensive support", "enhance capabilities") and omit numbers.
  - Collective sections list many items with no outcomes.
  - A few lines are copy-paste stubs.

## 5. Does Gabe's rule match?

- **3 sentences for a small initiative:** matches the floor (p10 = 3), but not the norm. The authors' median is 6 sentences (about 140 words), and 4 to 6 is the typical small entry. Entries at exactly 3 sentences are mostly list-of-events items (SEUK, Open CDMS), which are the ones that read as thin. Suggest **4 sentences minimum**, aiming for 5 to 6.
- **3 paragraphs for a large initiative (ParentText):** matches Chiara's practice (3 to 6 paragraphs, 190 to 400 words, 9 to 18 sentences). It does not match the authors overall (median 2 paragraphs, only 44% reach 3). Keep it for flagships, and add a sentence floor of about 10 sentences or 250 words, since paragraph counts are unreliable.
- **Better numbers (for agent-drafted descriptions):**

| Class | Sentences | Words | Paragraphs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 4 to 6 (floor 3) | 80 to 140 | 1 |
| Medium | 6 to 9 | 140 to 220 | 1 to 2 |
| Flagship | 10 to 15 | 250 to 400 | 3 or more |

- **Content checklist matters more than length:** why it matters and who it serves, where, what IDEMS did, what changed with at least one number, partners and funder, current status and date, one sentence of outlook. A 4-sentence entry that hits these beats a 12-sentence list of events.
- **Do not treat blank, stub or 1 to 2 sentence entries as the standard.** 124 of 432 entries in the working docs are under 15 words (about 29%), and 13 of 152 of the four authors' substantive entries are under 3 sentences.

## Method and limits

- **Sources read:**
  - IDEMS Activities 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 (Google Docs in the shared folder).
  - Annual Report 2022 (DS + DW), Annual Report 2020, CIC Report 2025.
  - Folder listing of the whole `IDEMS International` folder (2018 to 2025 annual reports, CIC reports, accounts).
- **Not read in full:** the other annual-report .docx/.pdf files, which are director-level summaries or CIC forms rather than entry-level. The 2025 CIC report only has four area paragraphs. The 2022 Annual Report narrative has no entry authorship.
- **Counts** are from my own script (432 headed entries across the five docs). Sentence splitting is regex-based and approximate (+/- 1). Paragraph counts treat bullet lines as paragraphs, which inflates a few entries (e.g. 23 or 28). Words are narrative text only, excluding names lines, status lines and links.
- **Attribution** is the capitalised-name rule above, so "wrote" is the doc's own convention and not independently verified.
- The 2024 and 2025 docs are drafts: 2025 has "ready for review" notes and many unwritten stubs, and some text there is a hand-off from earlier years.
